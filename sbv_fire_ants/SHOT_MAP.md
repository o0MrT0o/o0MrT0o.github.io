# Shot map — The Ants That Turn Themselves Into a Boat
All scenes must be authentic fire-ant footage or explicitly labeled scientific footage. No AI-generated or generic ant stock presented as proof.

00:00–00:12 | Cold open: a real raft on water, macro detail of ants gripping together. First line over the strongest raft image.
00:12–00:29 | Flood / problem: nest evacuation or raft at waterline. Do not imply a specific flood was filmed if footage is laboratory footage.
00:29–00:47 | Formation: source study footage showing the ball spreading into a raft; time-lapse may be used if labeled.
00:47–01:05 | Hydrophobic hairs / trapped air: close-up of ants at waterline and research footage; use a concise scientific overlay, not a fake animation.
01:05–01:24 | Two-layer structure: top-view and side-view research footage; show movement in both layers.
01:24–01:43 | Raft changing shape: source footage from the 2022 fluid-flow study; identify it as laboratory footage.
01:43–02:00 | Treadmilling / moving structural ants: time-lapse clearly labeled; do not present accelerated footage as real-time.
02:00–02:19 | Engineering significance: return to real footage and use only restrained typography for emergent behavior and no central controller.
02:19–02:38 | Hazard / queen and brood: only use clips where these are actually visible; do not infer unseen queen from generic raft footage.
02:38–02:55 | Payoff: wide raft shot, then close-up, end on “THE BOAT IS ALIVE.”
Target duration ~2:55 depending on Kokoro delivery. Irregular shot lengths; no fixed 3-second grid.

Source candidates:
1. Vernerey & White et al. (2025), “Dynamic self-organization in fire ant rafts underpins collective longevity and threat responsiveness,” Dryad DOI 10.5061/dryad.d51c5b0cv — associated SupplementaryVideos.zip. Dryad states datasets are CC0; credit is still recommended.
2. Ko et al. (2022), “Fire ant rafts elongate along fluid flows,” Bioinspiration & Biomimetics 17, 045007. Source footage / supplementary materials to be checked individually before use.
3. Mlot, Tovey & Hu (2011), “Fire ants self-assemble into waterproof rafts to survive floods,” PNAS 108:7669–7673, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1016658108. Excellent lab footage and evidence; verify reuse terms for each supplement before commercial publication.

No footage from PBS / BBC / National Geographic is authorized by this plan. Those pages are research references only, not downloadable-use permissions.
